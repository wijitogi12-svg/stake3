Mechanics

Questions about how staking actually works.

Many readers first need to understand the transaction validation model, delegation flow, and why token holders receive rewards at all. This page clarifies those basics before users move into forecasts or provider comparisons.

01

What is delegation?

Delegation lets token holders assign staking power to a validator without necessarily operating the infrastructure themselves, depending on the network design.

Open page →
02

Why are rewards paid?

Rewards compensate participants for helping secure the network, validate transactions, and maintain the protocol according to its consensus rules.

Open page →
03

Can I unstake instantly?

Usually not. Many networks impose an unbonding or withdrawal period that affects liquidity planning.

Risk

Questions about what can go wrong.

Educational staking content should always address the downside. Risk does not disappear because a reward figure looks attractive, and operational issues can matter as much as price movement.

04

Slashing

Some networks penalize validators for misbehavior or severe operational failure. Delegators should understand whether and how those penalties can flow through.

Open page →
05

Volatility

Reward income may not offset token price declines in the short term, which is why staking should be viewed within a broader portfolio context.

Open page →
06

Custody

Custodial staking can simplify access, but it changes the trust model and may affect control, withdrawals, and jurisdictional protections.

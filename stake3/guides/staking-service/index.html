Models

The main service paths available to stakers.

Staking-as-a-service usually means a specialized provider helps users access network rewards without requiring them to run validator infrastructure alone. The service can be exchange-based, institutional, non-custodial, or closer to direct delegation, depending on the product.

01

Custodial

The provider or exchange controls the operational flow and often the user experience, but that convenience changes the trust and withdrawal model.

Trade-offs

Convenience and control are usually in tension.

The reason many educational staking articles focus on service models is that a staking provider can simplify entry while also introducing dependencies that self-directed users must understand. Convenience can be valuable, but only when the trade-offs are visible and intentional.

07

Simplicity

Providers can lower the operational barrier for newcomers and reduce the complexity of direct validator management.
